# Portfolio

Malek Baghdadi's portfolio. Three pinned, scroll-driven demos (NeatInk, Pigeon, Logbook) followed by the rest of the CV.
Scrolling plays a scene; stopping stops it; scrolling back up rewinds it.

Design brief, the design critique and the storyboards: [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-05-portfolio-design.md`](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-05-portfolio-design.md).

## Run

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm run build      # -> dist/
npm run preview    # serve the production build
```

Vite, plain JavaScript modules, GSAP (ScrollTrigger). No framework. All CV text is real HTML in `index.html`.

## Motion

Everything that moves is tied to the scroll position (GSAP ScrollTrigger, smoothed by Lenis on wheel and trackpad;
touch keeps native scrolling), so stopping stops it and scrolling up plays it backwards. Motion is on unless a
visitor presses the page's own **Reduce motion** button (remembered in `localStorage`); the OS
`prefers-reduced-motion` flag is deliberately not used, because Windows often reports it with "Animation effects"
switched off by accident, and that silently turned every demo into a still on Malek's PC.

- `src/hero.js`: the opening. A highlighter runs under the thesis, the signal bars drop to "No connection" (the
  highlighter under "connection doesn't" turns flare and "connection" stutters), the name squeezes and its letters are
  let go one by one, tipping and drifting up, and the three apps rise out of the page onto their own contact shadows and
  light up, a light running across each screen ("All three work without a connection."). They lie back down as
  NeatInk slides over. At rest they float gently (paused while the page scrolls, so it costs nothing then).
- `src/scene.js`: shared by the three demos. The device stands up out of the page as a scene arrives (a light runs
  across its glass), the title opens to full width, caption words light up as you read, and each chapter slides over
  the previous one like a card while the old one is pushed back, dimmed and laid back down (sections overlap by one
  screen: `margin-top: -100svh`).
- `src/sections.js`: the CV sections. Headings open to full width, entries rise into place and their words light up as
  you read, the hero's highlighter runs under each title, the work timeline draws itself, skills arrive one by one, the
  marquee slides with the scroll and leans with its speed, the contact card grows to full width.
- Behind each scene, the app's world, on the same scroll timeline:
  - `src/hero-waves.js`: rings pulse out of the signal bars while the page is "online", break into dashes and die away
    as the connection drops; the clock's colon blinks. In the footer they come back with "Back online".
  - `src/scenes/pigeon-city.js`: a night skyline where the incident happens. A fire breaks out on the old market's second
    floor while the report is typed and takes the power with it (the city's lights stutter and go out, district by
    district). The broadcast pings out of it; the pigeons on the mosque take off and carry it across the city while
    phones light up in dark windows, nearest first. An ambulance and a fire truck arrive, headlights on, the ladder goes
    up and the water puts the fire out (the street stays wet, reflecting the fire and the sirens) while two phones
    merge; then the sirens stop, the ambulance leaves, the screens time out, the power comes back and two pigeons settle
    on the roof again. Thin clouds drift across the sky the whole way through, their undersides catching the fire's
    light while it burns. In the mesh each phone's screen flashes and shows the alert when the event reaches it, and
    goes to sleep when the radios go back to listening.
  - NeatInk (`src/scenes/neatink.js`): the scene's own ruled page, and the app's promises written large and faint
    ("offline", "no accounts", "no ads", generated by `tools/make-ink.mjs`) as the walkthrough goes; as each is
    finished the same hand marks it, the way notes get marked (underlined twice, ringed, starred). On the tablet,
    Convert runs a light along each written line and the ink turns into type as it passes (the type forms in step, so a
    word is never there twice), with the same light crossing the whole scene; "Fix all" lands with a burst of sparkles;
    the notes page slides away for the PDF; and the export ends "Saved Chapter 4.pdf".
  - `src/scenes/logbook-streaks.js`: a year of habit squares lying in perspective, filling in day by day, with the tally
    in the corner ("214 days logged, 12 in a row") and the months named along its far edge, the one being filled lit.
    Around the phone (`src/scenes/logbook.js`): a light on the floor
    under it, a line filling down the chapter list from dot to dot, and rays turning behind it when a card is revealed.
  - The CV (`src/sections.js`, `src/runner.js`): section rules draw themselves, the marquee's squares turn, a pixel
    runner crosses Game development (jumping at blocks, coins popping into a tally, dust when it lands, clouds drifting
    behind, a flag going up at the end), and the page ends "Back online". Behind every entry is its year, large and
    faint in the display face (an outline, like the marquee's second row; "Now" for what is still going on): it opens
    to full width as the entry arrives and drifts slower than the text in front, which gives the page depth. Every
    list of more than one entry is a timeline, with a stop at each entry that lights as the dot reaches it. Education
    is counted the way Logbook counts days, a square per semester and per school year filling in, and each group of
    skills has a count that runs up as its items arrive.
- Phones: one screen of scroll is the small viewport (`100svh`, `src/viewport.js`), not `innerHeight`, and nothing
  refreshes when the address bar shows or hides. Measured in `innerHeight`, each time the bar moved the eleven-screen
  NeatInk pin (and every other one) changed length under the visitor's finger and the page jumped between sections.
- A few small loops keep a scene alive when the scroll stops (flames, smoke, sirens, stars, the clock): `src/ambient.js`.
  They run only while their chapter can be seen and only during their part of the story, never with motion off, and
  `?still` in the address freezes them (the screenshot checks use it). Everything else still follows the scroll.
- `src/autoscroll.js`: the floating **Auto-scroll** button scrolls the whole site at a slow, steady 0.18 screens a
  second (about three and a half minutes end to end). While it plays, a slider above the button sets the speed: slide
  up for faster, down for slower, from 0.25× to 4× with 1× in the middle (drag, arrow keys or the wheel; remembered in
  `localStorage`). The visitor's own wheel, touch, scroll keys, scrollbar or a link hands control straight back; at the
  end it offers **Watch again**. It scrolls natively once a frame, first in GSAP's tick and timed by the frame's own
  timestamp, so phones get even whole-pixel steps; it does not go through Lenis every frame, which used to rewrite
  Lenis's classes on `<html>` twice a frame.
- `src/game.js` and `src/game/bugrun.js`: **Play Bug Run**, the arcade key above the pixel runner in Game development,
  opens the runner's course up into a small game, right there in the page (no window over it, nothing locked). Jump
  the bugs (Space, ↑ or a tap; hold for a higher jump), grab the coins, bump the **?** blocks from below for five more,
  jump the low flies and run under the high ones. It speeds up every 500 points ("FASTER!"), and the sky turns from day
  to sunset, night and dawn as the score climbs. ✕ or Esc folds it back into the course. A press anywhere else on the
  page pauses it, and so does scrolling it away; a tap or Space carries on. The best score and the sound switch (off to
  start with; the blips are made with WebAudio, there are no sound files) are remembered in `localStorage`. It is built
  to stay light: its code (5 KB gzipped) is fetched only as a hand reaches for Play; it draws a canvas 112 pixels high
  and as wide as the course has room for (200 to 480), scaled up by whole device pixels so the pixels stay crisp; the
  game runs in fixed 120 Hz steps with pooled objects (no garbage while playing); and it draws only while it is open,
  on screen and running. Every gap the course lays out can be cleared: a bot plays it in `tools/game-test.mjs`.
- Small touches under a mouse: the nav and footer links draw their underlines in, buttons catch a light, an
  experience entry's dates slide over in blue, skills light up, and a soft light follows the mouse around the dark
  footer. The work timeline's dot sends out a ping as it rides down the rail.
- Things to press, in every part of the page. None of them follows the scroll, so each rides its own channel and
  leaves the scroll's alone: the CSS `translate` / `rotate` / `scale` properties (never the `transform` GSAP owns), or
  an extra input to a value the scroll already computes.
  - The hero (`src/hero.js`, `src/hero-play.js`, `src/hero-waves.js`): the signal bars send a ping, one bold ring out
    of the bars that squeezes each letter of the name as it crosses it (with the connection scrolled away, the ring
    breaks up a third of the way out and the bars shake their head); the clock flips to the visitor's own time and how
    far it is from Beirut's; the name's letters can be pulled like rubber, neighbours dragged along, and snap back;
    the three devices drift after the mouse, the phones further than the tablet.
  - `src/birds.js`: the Pigeon scene's bird, out of its scene. Leave the page alone at the top and one lands on the
    "l" of Malek (found from the letter's baseline and the font's ascender), fidgets, and leaves the moment a mouse
    comes near. The footer's **Back to the top, by pigeon** flies one along, mail in its beak, while the page runs back
    up, and it lands on the name when it gets there.
  - The CV (`src/sections.js`, `src/stickers.js`, `src/runner.js`): a pixel sticker beside every entry (pops in with
    the scroll, grey until pressed); the marquee can be grabbed and thrown, coasts to a stop and wraps forever (a third
    copy of each row; touch keeps vertical scrolling); skills that something on the page was built with are links to
    it, saying where under a mouse; "In progress" has a barber's-pole bar; the scroll's runner hops when tapped and his
    blocks bump; every big heading's letters hop as a mouse brushes over them.
  - The footer: its bars ping too and each contact link lights up as the ring passes.
  - With motion off none of the moving ones run, and `?still` keeps the birds away for the screenshot checks.

## How the scenes work

Each scene (`src/scenes/*.js`) builds its own markup and one GSAP timeline whose progress is the scroll position
(`src/scene.js` pins the stage and wires the timeline to scroll). Phones get a camera that zooms and pans.

| Scene | What it is | How it is made |
|---|---|---|
| NeatInk | Tablet walkthrough (write, Convert, autocorrect, undo, lasso restyle, PDF) | Rebuilt in code from the app's Material 3 theme. Handwriting is generated from Hershey centre-lines and drawn with `perfect-freehand` (`tools/make-ink.mjs`) |
| Pigeon | Report an incident, spread across a mesh, set-union merge, duty cycle | Rebuilt in code from the app's `MeshTheme` and sync logic |
| Logbook | The real app | Frames captured from a local build against the Firebase emulators, with invented data (`tools/lb/capture.mjs`), drawn to a canvas |

NeatInk and Pigeon are labelled on the page as walkthroughs, not screen recordings.

## Regenerating assets

```bash
node tools/make-ink.mjs        # NeatInk handwriting -> src/scenes/neatink-ink.json
node tools/lb/capture.mjs      # Logbook frames -> public/logbook/ + src/scenes/logbook-manifest.json
node tools/hero-shots.mjs      # the hero's three screens -> public/hero/ (dev server on :5199)
node tools/make-preview.mjs    # the whole site as one inline html file -> artifact/site.html
```

`capture.mjs` needs Java and `firebase-tools` (via `npx`), and expects the Logbook repo at `C:/Users/Malek Baghdadi/personal-tracker`
(see `tools/lb/env.mjs`). It only reads that repo, writes nothing to it, and never touches a real Firebase project.
All data in the capture is invented.

## Checking changes

With `npm run dev -- --port 5199` running:

```bash
node tools/shoot.mjs <label> <w> <h> <neatink|pigeon|logbook> 0,0.3,0.6,1   # screenshots + proves reverse playback matches
node tools/at.mjs <label> <w> <h> top@1 neatink@0@-0.55 experience@0       # the seams between sections
node tools/perf.mjs 390 844 neatink --cpu=4                                 # frame times while scrolling a scene
node tools/wheel.mjs                                                        # real wheel scrolling + the demos link
node tools/autoplay-test.mjs                                                # auto-scroll: pace, speed slider, take-over, keyboard, end, phone taps and even steps
node tools/end-state.mjs                                                    # nothing left half-animated at the page end
node tools/game-test.mjs                                                    # Bug Run in the page: opens in place, keys don't scroll, buttons, a bot plays, pause, close
node tools/eggs-test.mjs                                                    # the Easter eggs, found the way a visitor would, desktop and phone
```

`shoot.mjs` and `at.mjs` take `--motion=off` to check the still version. They use the system Chrome (set `CHROME`
to the path of another one). `shoot.mjs` loads the
page with `?still`, so the ambient loops (which follow time, not scroll) cannot spoil its reverse-playback comparison.

## Easter eggs

The page never announces them: no notes, no tally, nothing kept. Each one simply does what it does.

<details>
<summary>Spoilers: what's hidden in the page</summary>

- **The signal bars** (top left, or the footer's). Press them: a ping rings out and the name's letters squeeze as it
  passes.
- **The clock.** Press it for your own time, and how far that is from Beirut's.
- **Click the letters of the name.** Each one hops; hop all thirteen and the whole name does a wave.
- **Pull a letter of the name** with a mouse or a pen. Rubber.
- **Wait at the top.** After nine quiet seconds a pigeon lands on the "l". Go near it.
- **Write on NeatInk's page**, or on the tablet, with a mouse or a pen. The ink fades away like the scene's own.
- **Write a little more, and stop.** A light runs along what you wrote and it is "converted" to type. (It can't read.
  It says so.)
- **Poke a pigeon** sitting on the mosque in the Pigeon scene. (Wide screens: on a phone they sit behind the phone.)
- **Click the moon** over Pigeon's city: a shooting star, and the moon moves on a phase.
- **Click the city's windows.** Lights go on and off; they are yours, so they stay on through the power cut.
- **Click Logbook's floor.** A day is logged under the mouse, and the tally in the corner counts it.
- **Press the CV's stickers.** They keep their colours.
- **Throw the marquee.**
- **Type "coo"** anywhere on the page. A flock crosses with the mail.
- **Type "offline"** anywhere on the page. It blinks out like a dropped signal, and comes straight back.
- **↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ ← → ← → B A.** Coins rain down the screen, and Bug Run's runner turns gold (and stays gold).
- **The console.** Open the dev tools: a hello, with a hint.
- **Leave the tab.** Its title loses the connection ("📡 Connection lost… come back?") and is "✅ Back online" when you
  come back.

Most are in `src/eggs.js`; the rest live with what they play with (`hero.js`, `hero-play.js`, `birds.js`,
`stickers.js`, `sections.js`, `scenes/logbook-streaks.js`). `tools/eggs-test.mjs` finds each one the way a visitor
